## 2. Decisions applied, and what applying them revealed

### C1 — Tenure→rate mapping · **partially resolved**

**Applied.** A `tenureRates: { tenureMonths, annualRate }[]` mapping is added to
Booking's own `DepositTerms`; `resolveTenureRate(terms, tenureMonths)` becomes
the single owner of "what rate does this tenure earn". Marketplace is untouched.

**The consequence, stated plainly.** The previous plan proposed shipping *"a
plausible rate card marked temporary, as T3.1 did with its fixture."* The
decision **"Booking must not invent rates"** withdraws that, and the withdrawal
has a visible cost:

> With no rate data, the platform can honestly offer **one** tenure — the 24
> months the product already quotes at 7.25%. The approved screen draws
> **seven** tenure chips plus a slider.

There is no third option. Any tenure without a rate either quotes a return the
user cannot obtain (which C1 forbids and M2.6 already refused) or is invented
(which C1 forbids explicitly).

**Interim behaviour proposed** — honest, and reversible the moment data arrives:

- Render only tenures present in `tenureRates`
- The slider's domain is the mapped tenures, not the full `tenureRange`
- With a one-entry card the tenure control renders as a single selected chip,
  read-only, with the slider suppressed rather than shown at zero width

**Still required from Product:** the tenure→rate card for at least the demo
product. This is a **data deliverable, not a decision** — a table of
`{ tenureMonths, annualRate }`. Seven rows unblocks the approved screen exactly
as drawn.

### C2 — Platform calculation engine · **partially resolved, engineering unblocked**

**Applied.** Booking uses the platform engine — quarterly compounding, derived
in M2.1 and matched against all six published Marketplace figures. No value is
reverse-engineered from the artwork. The artwork discrepancy remains an open
Product/Design clarification and is **not** a code dependency.

**The consequence.** The screen will visibly disagree with the design deck:

| | Maturity | Total interest |
|---|---|---|
| Platform engine (what ships) | **₹5,77,270** | ₹77,270 |
| Approved artwork | ₹5,66,478 | ₹66,478 |
| Divergence | **₹10,792** | |

This is correct behaviour and a known visual divergence. It matters because the
founder demo will be walked against the design deck — see **R-F5**. Recorded so
it is discovered in this document rather than in the room.

### C3 — Absolute quick amounts · **resolved, two sub-questions raised**

**Applied.** Chips set the investment amount. Additive behaviour is removed from
the plan entirely.

**Corroboration worth noting.** The decision is supported by the artwork's own
state: `+ ₹5L` is drawn *selected* while the amount field reads ₹5,00,000. That
is exactly what an absolute chip looks like after being pressed, and is a state
an additive chip should never hold. The approved decision and the artwork agree
on behaviour; only the label disagrees.

**C3a — the label.** Rendering `+ ₹5L` on a control that *sets* rather than adds
is actively misleading on a money field. Proposed: render **`₹1L` `₹5L` `₹10L`**
and log the dropped `+` as the UI inconsistency the decision asks us to record.
*Confirm the label may lose its `+`.*

**C3b — one of the three chips is below the product minimum.** Discovered while
applying the decision:

| Chip | Absolute value | Product minimum | Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `+ ₹1L` | ₹1,00,000 | ₹5,00,000 | ❌ **below minimum** |
| `+ ₹5L` | ₹5,00,000 | ₹5,00,000 | ✅ exactly the minimum |
| `+ ₹10L` | ₹10,00,000 | ₹5,00,000 | ✅ |

Under additive semantics this never surfaced. Under absolute semantics, pressing
the first chip puts the form into a guaranteed BR-INV-001 violation — a control
whose only function is to produce an error. Options:

- **A (recommended)** — derive quick amounts from the product's own range, so
  they are always valid for the product on screen
- **B** — keep the fixed three and disable those outside the range. Honest, but
  a permanently dead chip on the demo product
- **C** — keep the fixed three and clamp to the minimum. Rejected: a control
  that silently does something other than its label is worse than one that errs

Also unresolved by C3: spec 21b §B lists **five** chips (₹50K, ₹1L, ₹2L, ₹5L,
₹10L), the artwork draws **three**. Under the stated priority order Product
Analysis (2) outranks Approved UI (3), which would mean five. Recommend
following the artwork's three for visual fidelity and logging the count as part
of the same inconsistency. *Confirm.*

### C4 — Cumulative and non-cumulative · **resolved**

**Applied.** Two payout models, and the maturity figure means different things
in each:

| Payout | Interest basis | Maturity Amount | Total Interest | Per-instalment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At Maturity / Cumulative | Quarterly compounding | ₹5,77,270 | ₹77,270 | — |
| Monthly | Simple, paid monthly | **₹5,00,000** | ₹72,500 | ₹3,020.83 / month |
| Quarterly | Simple, paid quarterly | **₹5,00,000** | ₹72,500 | ₹9,062.50 / quarter |

*"Never display compounded maturity for monthly payout products"* is now a
**verification invariant**, not just a rule — a probe will assert that a
non-cumulative selection returns `maturityValue === principal`.

One open detail: the per-instalment figure is not in the approved artwork.
Showing it is the only way "Monthly" communicates anything the user can act on,
so it is planned as a fourth line under the returns strip. *Confirm.*

### C5 — Custom tenure · **resolved**

**Applied.** `Custom` reveals a **numeric month input**, constrained to the
product's mapped tenure range. No dialog, no date picker. The artwork's calendar
icon is logged as a UI inconsistency requiring confirmation — a calendar picks a
date, and tenure is a duration.

Note the dependency: Custom tenure is only meaningful once a rate exists for the
month entered, so its usefulness is bounded by **C1**.

### C6 — Post-tax · **resolved**

Booking exposes the integration seam (`taxProfile?` on the returns derivation)
and makes **no tax assumption**. No post-tax figure renders until the goal-led
planner supplies a bracket. See **R-F4** for the demo consequence.

### C7 — Four phases, eight routes · **resolved, implemented**

Delivered in T3.2.1 and covered by unit and invariant assertions. No further
work. See **R-F2** for the UX risk this reconciliation carries.

### 3.3 Re-sequenced

The previous order built controls before the calculation they feed. The new
order is **engine → model → validation → shell → controls**, so every control is
wired to something already proven:

```
T3.2.4  maturity engine at platform level        ← no UI, unit-testable
T3.2.5  payout-aware returns model               ← no UI, unit-testable
T3.2.6  validation schema + plain-English copy   ← no UI, unit-testable
        ─── review gate ───
T3.2.7  screen shell + configured right rail     ← first visible screen
T3.2.9  payout control                           ← the C4 model, made visible
T3.2.8  amount field + quick amounts + returns   ← pending C3a/C3b
        ─── review gate ───
T3.2.10 tenure selector                          ← waits on rate data
T3.2.12 navigation, validation seam, audit
```

**Two changes worth naming.** The tenure selector moves from the middle to the
end, so the one data-blocked task cannot stall anything behind it. And the
payout control (T3.2.9) now precedes the amount field, because it is the control
that proves the C4 model on screen and it carries no open questions.

The screen is demonstrable after T3.2.9 with tenure fixed at 24 months — which
is exactly what M2.6 shipped for the Marketplace calculator, and defensible for
the same reason.

## 5. C8 — architectural recommendation: converging on a shared finance utility

### 5.1 The problem

`calculateMaturity` lives in `src/modules/marketplace/utils/maturity.ts`. Booking
cannot import it — the module boundary is ESLint-enforced and correct. CLAUDE.md
requires promotion over duplication. Priority 4 forbids modifying Marketplace
without explicit instruction. All three cannot hold at once.

### 5.2 Interim — accepted, with the risk contained

`@/utils/maturity.ts` is added as a **new platform file** (adding a platform file
is not modifying Marketplace). Booking imports it. Marketplace keeps its copy.

**Containment:** T3.2.4 ships a unit assertion that runs *both* implementations
over all six published Marketplace figures and fails the build if they ever
disagree. The duplication cannot drift silently — it can only be deleted or
detected.

### 5.3 Registered technical debt

> **TD-001 — Duplicate maturity calculation**
> **Incurred** 2026-08-06, T3.2.4 · **Reason** Marketplace no-touch constraint
> **Interest** Every future change to the formula must be made twice, correctly,
> or the build fails. Rate slabs, TDS and non-cumulative payouts are all
> foreseeable changes.
> **Principal** ~40 lines plus one type pair.
> **Trigger to repay** the first change to either implementation, the first
> platform-level finance need from a third module (Portfolio), or explicit
> instruction — whichever is first.

### 5.4 Target — `src/utils/finance/` as the platform finance layer

Not a Booking utility promoted upward, but a platform capability that Marketplace,
Booking, Portfolio, Reports and the future FD Ladder all consume:

```
src/utils/finance/
  maturity.ts       calculateMaturity, calculateInterestEarned
  payout.ts         cumulative vs non-cumulative (from T3.2.5)
  rates.ts          resolveApplicableRate (amount slabs, from Marketplace)
                    resolveTenureRate    (tenure card,  from Booking)
  tax.ts            post-tax estimation  (when C6's planner lands)
src/types/finance.ts   MaturityInput, MaturityResult, RateSlab, TenureRate
```

Rationale: **money rules are platform vocabulary, not feature logic.** Document
19 §2 gives every business entity exactly one authoritative location; the same
principle applied to calculation says the platform must have one answer to "what
is this deposit worth". The FD Ladder in Founder §6 will need maturity, payout
*and* rate resolution simultaneously across several banks — it cannot import
them from Marketplace, and it must not own a third copy.

### 5.5 Refactor plan — `R1`, one reviewable change

1. Move `maturity.ts` and `rate-slabs.ts` into `src/utils/finance/`; move their
   types to `@/types/finance`
2. Re-point Marketplace's imports. **No logic changes** — this is the only step
   that touches Marketplace, and it touches import lines only
3. Delete the interim platform copy and the cross-implementation assertion,
   which will have served its purpose
4. Re-run the existing Marketplace unit suite unchanged — the six published
   figures are the regression test, and they already exist
5. Add a boundary probe: no module may declare a finance calculation of its own

**Estimate** half a day. **Blast radius** import statements in roughly four
Marketplace files. **Rollback** revert one commit.

**Recommended timing:** before Portfolio (Founder §8 Week 4). Portfolio is the
third consumer, and three copies is where this stops being cheap to fix.

**Requires:** explicit instruction to modify Marketplace. Not taken.
