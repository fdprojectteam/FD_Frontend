Step 1 — Business Context

This document defines why the Booking module exists.

1. Problem Statement

Users have already selected an FD after comparing products.

However, completing the investment often becomes the most difficult part of the journey.

Traditional booking experiences involve:

Multiple forms
Repeated personal information
Confusing banking terminology
Long verification processes
Unclear progress
Unexpected validation errors
Uncertainty about what happens next

Instead of increasing confidence, the booking journey often introduces doubt at the final stage.

For a financial product, this is the worst possible moment to create friction.

2. Opportunity

Booking is more than collecting user information.

It is the final opportunity to reinforce trust before the user commits their money.

A well-designed booking experience should:

reassure users that they made the right choice,
explain every important step,
prevent avoidable mistakes,
minimize effort,
make the investment feel safe and straightforward.

The goal is not just successful form submission—it is confident investment completion.

3. Product Vision

The Booking module should function as an investment completion assistant, not merely a sequence of forms.

It should help users:

confirm their chosen FD,
review critical investment details,
provide only essential information,
understand the implications of their choices,
complete the investment quickly and securely.

The experience should remove uncertainty while maintaining transparency.

4. Business Objectives

The Booking module should support measurable business outcomes:

Increase booking completion rate.
Reduce abandonment during onboarding.
Reduce customer support related to booking.
Improve user trust and satisfaction.
Minimize booking errors and failed submissions.
Enable future expansion to additional investment products.
5. User Objectives

Users want to:

complete the investment quickly,
avoid making costly mistakes,
know exactly what they are investing in,
understand what happens after submission,
feel confident that their money is secure,
avoid entering the same information repeatedly.
6. Product Principles

Every Booking decision should satisfy these principles:

Trust First

The interface should reassure users at every step.

Examples:

secure messaging,
verified partner information,
transparent review before confirmation.
Minimal Effort

Ask only for information that is genuinely required.

Whenever possible:

pre-fill,
reuse,
infer,
remember.
Transparency

Every action should answer:

Why is this information needed?
What will happen next?
Can I edit this later?
Progressive Disclosure

Show information only when it becomes relevant.

Avoid overwhelming users with large forms or unnecessary details.

Error Prevention

Prevent mistakes before submission instead of displaying errors afterward.

Examples:

inline validation,
smart defaults,
contextual guidance.
Confidence Before Confirmation

Before users submit, they should clearly understand:

investment amount,
tenure,
maturity value,
nominee,
payout preference,
linked bank account,
next steps.

The confirmation screen should feel like a final reassurance rather than just another form.

7. Success Statement

The Booking experience is successful when a first-time investor can complete an FD investment without confusion, without external assistance, and with complete confidence that they understand what they are investing in.
